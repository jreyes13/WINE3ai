The 3 Global Gold Standards of Sommellerie

WINEp3dIA

Professional sommelier education, enological biochemistry, and terroir intelligence structured under the 3 global gold standards (WSET 3, CMS, and ASI), augmented with the definitive treatises of Hugh Johnson & Jancis Robinson, Jamie Goode, Pascaline Lepeltier, and the VIVC international ampelographic catalogue.

01 · Academic Certification

WSET 3

Wine & Spirit Education Trust

The international academic benchmark. Details how natural factors (climate, soils, aspect) and human factors determine wine style, quality, and price.

Key Tool: SAT Systematic Approach to Tasting: calibrated scoring of color, acidity, tannin, alcohol, and finish.
02 · Deductive Tasting Mastery

CMS

Court of Master Sommeliers

The pinnacle of blind deductive tasting and hospitality service. Strict formal logic where sensory evidence systematically deduces variety, origin, and vintage.

Key Tool: Deductive Grid Sight (rim, tears) ➔ Nose (condition, earth, oak) ➔ Palate (structure) ➔ Final Deduction.
03 · Global Standard & Service

ASI

Association de la Sommellerie Int.

Global federation uniting sommelier associations across +60 countries. Establishes deontological codes, precision cellar service, temperatures, and the World's Best Sommelier contest.

Key Tool: Floor Protocol Universal serving temperatures, candle decanting etiquette, and table psychology.

Terroir Profile #02

Ribera del Duero

Castile and León · Northern Meseta
Extreme Continental Climate: Harsh winters and dry summers with diurnal temperature swings of up to 20°C (36°F) that preserve fresh acidity in the grape skins.
High Elevation: Vineyards planted between 750 and over 900 meters (2,450–3,000 ft) above sea level on fluvial terraces and limestone moors.
Diverse Soils: Alternating layers of calcareous marl, chalky sands, sedimentary clay, and alluvial gravel terraces.

Tasting Matrix #03

CMS Deductive Tasting

Formal Blind Deductive Sequence
1
Sight: Clarity, core color, rim variation, viscosity & tears.
2
Nose: Condition, intensity, fruit (fresh/tart/jammy), floral, earth/mineral, oak impact.
3
Palate: Dryness, acidity level, tannin texture (fine/grainy), alcohol, body, and persistent finish.
4
Conclusion: Climate (cool/moderate/warm), grape variety, Old vs New World, country, region, vintage estimate.

Academic Sources & Official References

WINE³ai is an educational, non-commercial research platform dedicated to terroir intelligence. We explicitly acknowledge the leadership and official canons established by the world's benchmark wine education institutions:

Cartography & Ampelography United Kingdom / Global

The World Atlas of Wine & Wine Grapes

Hugh Johnson & Jancis Robinson, MW

The supreme cartographic benchmark for world terroirs and the definitive guide to 1,368 vine varieties (co-authored with Julia Harding and José Vouillamoz). Foundational source for regional boundaries, macroclimates, and genetic classifications.

🔗 jancisrobinson.com → Mitchell Beazley / Penguin Random House.
Official Genetics Germany / JKI

Vitis International Variety Catalogue (VIVC)

Julius Kühn-Institut (JKI) · Geilweilerhof

The official ampelographic database of the Federal Research Centre for Cultivated Plants (Germany). Global authority for genetic cultivar verification, pedigree validation, and international synonyms of Vitis.

🔗 vivc.de / Official Database → Federal Research Centre for Cultivated Plants (JKI).
Botany & Sommellerie France / USA

One Thousand Vines: A New Way to Understand Wine

Pascaline Lepeltier, MS · Meilleur Sommelier de France

Contemporary masterpiece synthesizing botany, deep geology, soil physics, and dynamic living sensory tasting. Essential analytical framework for vine-root-microbiome interactions and dining lipid dynamics.

🔗 pascalinelepeltier.com → Mitchell Beazley / Hachette Book Group.
Enological Biochemistry UC Press

The Science of Wine: From Vine to Glass (2nd Ed.)

Jamie Goode, PhD in Plant Biology

The modern scientific benchmark covering aroma chemistry (thiols, pyrazines, rotundone, TDN), fermentation microbiology, sulfites, oxidation, and the neurobiological mechanics of human olfactory perception.

🔗 wineanorak.com / Jamie Goode → University of California Press.
